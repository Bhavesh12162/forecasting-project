Tata Motors India volumes were up 40.9% year on year by Q2 FY27, and simple forecasts lagged behind the jump

Twenty-two quarters of wholesale volumes (Q1 FY22 to Q2 FY27), tested against four forecasting methods.

Company snapshot

Tata Motors' India auto business makes commercial vehicles (CV: trucks, buses, small commercial vehicles) and passenger vehicles (PV: cars and SUVs, including EVs). In 2025 the two were split into separate listed companies, so this report adds CV and PV together to keep one continuous series. Figures are wholesale dispatches from the company, not retail registrations. Jaguar Land Rover is not included.

What the past data shows

Quarterly total volume (units)

Q2 FY27 (336,837) is the highest quarter in the dataset. The dip to 210,415 in Q1 FY26 came right before the climb.

CV and PV volume by quarter (units)

Both segments rose together from Q3 FY26, with PV supplying roughly 60% of volume. FY22 has no split in the source data.

Year-on-year growth by quarter (%)

Growth stayed between -12% and +5% from Q1 FY24 to Q1 FY26, then reached +11.2%, +21.6%, +32.1%, +38.3% and +40.9% in the last five quarters. The FY23 figures compare against a weak FY22 base.

EV units and EV share of PV

EV volume fell 12.9% in FY25, then rose 43.3% in FY26 to 92,120, which is 14.4% of PV. EV data exists only for FY22, FY24, FY25 and FY26.

Why forecasting matters in automobile operations

Vehicle plants plan production, parts orders and shifts weeks ahead. A forecast that is too low means lost sales and overtime. One that is too high means unsold stock and holding cost. Every method below is scored on the same quarters (t = 5 to 22, Q1 FY23 to Q2 FY27) so the scores can be compared fairly.

Results

Recommendations

  1. Plan production with a weighted average of the last four quarters. It gave the lowest MAPE and the lowest RMSE of the four methods tested.
  2. Size safety stock from forecast error, not a rule of thumb. Safety stock = z x RMSE x square root of lead time in quarters.
  3. Re-check the forecast every quarter and reset after a jump. Averaging methods lag when volume moves fast, as the last six quarters show.
  4. Build extra capacity ahead of Q4. Q4 has carried the largest share of annual volume in every year.
  5. Forecast PV, CV and EV separately. Their growth rates differ (FY26: CV +13.6%, PV +15.3%, EV +43.3%).

Effect of the recommendations

Assumptions. Errors are measured over t = 5 to 22 using the same data. Safety stock assumes forecast errors are roughly normal and independent between quarters. Holding cost is your own input; no number is assumed. These are estimated impacts, not guaranteed savings.

Roadmap, risks and limits

Roadmap

  • Now: switch the planning forecast to the weighted 4-quarter average
  • Next quarter: compare forecast with actual dispatches
  • Within two quarters: set safety stock from measured error
  • Ongoing: split forecasts by PV, CV and EV

Risks and fixes

  • Surge fades: review monthly, keep flexible shifts
  • Supplier delays: share the forecast range, not one number
  • Model drift: re-score every quarter

Limits of this study

  • Only 22 quarters, with a structural jump near the end
  • Wholesale, not retail, volumes
  • FY23 split and Q1 FY27 are partly derived
